# Academic Integrity and AI Use

## What counts as academic misconduct at ANU
- **Plagiarism.** Using another person's words, ideas, code, or data without attribution.
- **Collusion.** Working together on individual assessments beyond what is permitted.
- **Contract cheating.** Paying or asking someone else (including a tutor or family member) to do your work.
- **Recycling.** Submitting the same work for multiple assessments without permission.
- **Falsification.** Fabricating data, references, or experimental results.
- **Misuse of generative AI.** Using ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, etc., in ways the course outline does not permit.

## AI use — read your course outline
ANU does **not** have a single university-wide rule for generative AI. Each course outline specifies what is allowed. Typical categories:

- **AI prohibited.** No AI assistance at all.
- **AI as tutor.** AI for explanation and study, but not in submitted work.
- **AI as collaborator.** AI usage permitted for drafting, with attribution.
- **AI as required tool.** Some assignments require AI use.

If unsure → ask the convener **in writing** and keep the reply.

## Attribution when AI is permitted
When a course allows AI use, you usually need to:

- State which tool you used (model and version).
- Describe how you used it (brainstorming, drafting, code review, etc.).
- Keep the prompts and outputs available if asked.

A short methodology note is enough — a paragraph in the assignment, not a full appendix unless required.

## What happens if you are reported
1. The course convener reviews the suspected breach.
2. If there is a case to answer, it is referred to the **College's Associate Dean (Education)** or the Academic Misconduct process.
3. You will be notified in writing and have an opportunity to respond.
4. Outcomes range from re-do of the assessment, mark adjustment, fail of the assessment or course, through to formal academic misconduct on your record.
5. You can be supported through the process by the **ANUSA Student Assistance** team — free, confidential, independent of the university.

## If you have made a mistake
- Do not panic and do not delete or edit anything after the submission.
- Contact **ANUSA Student Assistance** before responding — they will help you prepare a statement.
- Be honest with the process. Concealment makes outcomes worse; cooperation often improves them.

## Practical guidance
- Keep notes and drafts. If you are accused, being able to show your process is the strongest defence.
- For group work, agree in writing on what each person contributes.
- Use a citation manager (Zotero, EndNote) to avoid accidental plagiarism.
- When using AI, save the conversation. If permitted, you can show it; if not, you have a record of what you did.
