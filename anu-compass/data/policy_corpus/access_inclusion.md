# Access and Inclusion (EAP — Education Access Plan)

## What it is
An **Education Access Plan (EAP)** is a formal arrangement between you and the university that records reasonable adjustments to your study because of a disability, ongoing health condition, mental-health condition, or carer responsibilities.

Common adjustments include:
- Extra time in exams.
- Rest breaks during exams.
- Separate / quiet exam room.
- Extension flexibility built into the plan.
- Note-taker or audio recording permission for lectures.
- Adjusted attendance requirements.
- Alternative assessment arrangements.

## Who is eligible
- Students with a **disability** as defined under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992.
- Ongoing or recurring **medical conditions** affecting study (chronic illness, autoimmune, severe migraines, etc.).
- **Mental-health conditions** (anxiety, depression, ADHD, PTSD, etc.) — diagnosis required from a qualified practitioner.
- **Primary carers** of someone with significant support needs.
- Some short-term conditions can also have a temporary plan (e.g., recovery from surgery for a semester).

## How to apply
1. Gather supporting documentation from a qualified practitioner — must describe the condition's **functional impact** on study, not just diagnosis.
2. Apply through `my.anu.edu.au` → Access and Inclusion → New Plan.
3. Book a meeting with an Access and Inclusion adviser (in person, phone, or Zoom).
4. The adviser drafts a plan; you review and sign.
5. The plan is then visible to your course conveners — you do not have to negotiate adjustments separately.

Allow **2–4 weeks** from application to a finalised plan, longer near semester start. Apply *before* census date if you can.

## What the EAP does *not* cover
- Specific deadline extensions for individual assessments — those still go through the normal Extension or Special Consideration channels (the EAP can make those easier to get).
- Reductions in core academic standards — adjustments cannot lower the academic requirements of a course.
- Personal counselling (use ANU Counselling).
- Financial support (use the Financial Support pathway).

## Tips
- Be specific in your medical letter: ask the practitioner to write about *what you cannot do without adjustment*, not just diagnosis.
- A plan early is better than a plan late — apply at the start of semester.
- You can update a plan at any time if your situation changes.
- Information in your EAP is **confidential**; conveners only see the adjustments, not the underlying condition.

## Contact
Access and Inclusion is the central team. They are usually based in the Student Services area. Bookings via `my.anu.edu.au`.
